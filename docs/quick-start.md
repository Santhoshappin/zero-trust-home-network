# Quick start: the 15-minute version

No time for the full [runbook](runbook.md)? Do these six things today. They remove the most common home-network risks for anyone working from home.

| # | Do this | Why | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Change the router admin password** (log in at the address on the router sticker, often `192.168.1.1` or `192.168.0.1`) | Factory passwords are often printed on the box or published online | 2 min |
| 2 | **Update the router firmware**, or turn on auto-update | Old router firmware is one of the most exploited devices on the internet | 3 min |
| 3 | **Turn off WPS and UPnP** | WPS can be brute-forced; UPnP lets any device open ports to the internet | 2 min |
| 4 | **Enable the guest Wi-Fi**, give it its own password, and turn **client isolation** on (if the option exists) | This becomes your **work network** | 3 min |
| 5 | **Move the work laptop to that network**, then *forget* the home Wi-Fi on the laptop | Your TV, cameras and kids' devices can no longer reach it | 2 min |
| 6 | **Keep the corporate VPN on** for all work | The home network becomes just a pipe to the internet | 1 min |

Then, when you have 10 more minutes, **check that it works**: from the work laptop, try to ping one home device (for example the TV or printer). It should **fail**. See [verification.md](verification.md).

> Can't find these settings, or your router has no guest network? See [router-guide.md](router-guide.md).
> Something stopped working? See [limits-and-faq.md](limits-and-faq.md).

**Tell your IT department.** Some employers have rules about home networks, and most will appreciate that you asked.
